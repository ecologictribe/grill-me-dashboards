The 20 Challenges — Pick 3

The Hero Axis

Easy
📖 Scenario: Your school's monthly attendance bar chart starts its y-axis at 85% so the improvement "looks dramatic".
🤺 Grill seed: "What does hiding the bottom 85 points of context let someone claim — and what does zero-baselining cost you instead?"
2

Red Means Go?

Easy
📖 Scenario: The physics lab equipment board shows "in use" in green and "faulty" in red. Nothing else distinguishes them.
🤺 Grill seed: "What does a color-blind student see on this board — and what two-token fix makes status unmissable?"
3

The Missing Tuesday

Easy
📖 Scenario: A daily step-count line chart fills a sensor outage day with a dip to zero — readers think nobody walked on Tuesday.
🤺 Grill seed: "Where did the data go, and what on-chart treatment shows 'unknown' instead of 'zero'?"
4

Pie Overload

Easy
📖 Scenario: The canteen budget pie has 14 slices. Nobody can compare two of them.
🤺 Grill seed: "Which 3 slices does your actual audience care about — and what chart type lets them compare?"
5

The Zoomed Breakout

Easy
📖 Scenario: The chess club shows "explosive growth" zoomed to the last 3 weeks of a flat 3-year membership record.
🤺 Grill seed: "What is the full range, how do you show it, and what annotation makes the zoom honest?"
6

Which Average?

Easy
📖 Scenario: A class-marks chart is badged "Class average: 78" — but two toppers skewed the mean; the typical student scored 64.
🤺 Grill seed: "Mean or median for this story, what does your tooltip say, and who is misled if you pick wrong?"
7

Double-Ruler Drama

Medium
📖 Scenario: The school transport dashboard plots bus on-time % (0–100) and diesel cost (₹ thousands) on dual axes — the lines cross "proving" spending causes delays.
🤺 Grill seed: "What argument does the crossing invite, and what layout makes that argument impossible to fake?"
8

Silent Row Loss

Medium
📖 Scenario: The recycling-drive tally imports a CSV into a spreadsheet that stops at 1,000 rows. The dashboard shows a confident, wrong total.
🤺 Grill seed: "At which hop (capture → import → dashboard) can rows vanish, and what count assertion fires when they do?"
9

Hue-Only Heat Map

Medium
📖 Scenario: A room-usage heat map encodes busy-ness by one blue's intensity only — printed in grayscale, it's unreadable.
🤺 Grill seed: "What non-color redundancy (labels, hatch, numbers) carries the same message when hue is stripped?"
10

The Flicker Feed

Medium
📖 Scenario: A live canteen-queue counter repaints on every ping — the screen flickers and students stop reading it.
🤺 Grill seed: "What aggregation window and repaint cap make this stream readable at a glance?"
11

Mobile Mashup

Medium
📖 Scenario: The 6-panel exam-results dashboard designed on a lab monitor gets opened on parents' phones at PTM night. Everything is mush.
🤺 Grill seed: "What is the ONE question a phone must answer, and what survives to the smallest screen?"
12

Hover-Only Facts

Medium
📖 Scenario: Units and date ranges live only in tooltips. The moment the dashboard is screenshotted for the prospectus, the context evaporates.
🤺 Grill seed: "What must be visible in a static screenshot for the chart to stay truthful?"
13

Two Vote Counts

Medium
📖 Scenario: Science-fair voting: one sheet counts 3 votes per student, another counts 1. Both dashboards are "official".
🤺 Grill seed: "What is the single governed definition of a vote, where is it displayed, and who owns it?"
14

Names on the Big Screen

Medium
📖 Scenario: The fitness-test dashboard lists every student's name and score — projected in the hall during the event.
🤺 Grill seed: "Who should see whose number? What aggregation or opt-in makes this display ethical and legal?"
15

Cluster Mirage

Hard
📖 Scenario: A 2D "activity map" of students shows two pretty islands. The school wants to declare two personality types from it.
🤺 Grill seed: "What evidence beyond the 2D projection would confirm these clusters — and how does the chart communicate doubt?"
16

Alarm Storm

Hard
📖 Scenario: Sports-day operations dashboard flashes ~20 alerts per minute. Officials now ignore all of them — including the real one.
🤺 Grill seed: "Which condition genuinely deserves an alert, what's the threshold, and where does the calm baseline live?"
17

The Sarcasm Meter

Hard
📖 Scenario: Event-feedback sentiment scores "Oh great, another drill 😍" as 100% positive. The organizers conclude the drill was a hit.
🤺 Grill seed: "Where is the uncertainty in a sentiment score, and what sample quotes keep the meter honest?"
18

Phantom Spike

Hard
📖 Scenario: The bus-GPS live view shows a speed "spike" — it's actually the per-second feed colliding with a daily-log average on the same chart.
🤺 Grill seed: "Which clock wins, what shared window reconciles the feeds, and how is each series' cadence labelled?"
19

Broken Inheritance

Hard
📖 Scenario: Last year's prize-dashboard hardcodes "2024" in five places. This year it quietly shows wrong year-on-year comparisons.
🤺 Grill seed: "What is data-driven vs hardcoded, and what single-source-of-truth pattern makes next year safe?"
20

Export Amnesia

Hard
📖 Scenario: The interactive admission dashboard is flattened to PDF for the board pack — every hover-card insight is gone; only pictures remain.
🤺 Grill seed: "What is essential when flattened, and how do you design every view to survive being an image?"

🎤 Presentation Day — 5 Nov 2026

Every student (or pair) presents their three reports. 5–7 minutes each — slots confirmed by the trainer. Suggested running order:

  1. The challenge and its real-world parent (30 sec per report).
  2. The grill: show one real interview round — question, recommended default, your approval (1 min per report).
  3. The spec decisions your dashboard visibly obeys — point at pixels, cite D-numbers (1 min per report).
  4. The gates: name them, show them passing (HTML: grader output; Power BI: your hand-checked screenshots).
  5. Your self-score vs the trainer's — and defend the delta when asked.
Expect These Three Viva Questions
  1. "Show me the decision behind this pixel." — point to the spec line that produced it.
  2. "Which gate would have caught this if I'd drifted?" — name the boolean check your artifact passes and a lazy version would fail.
  3. "Why did you score yourself that?" — defend your number with evidence, not vibes.

You cannot fail the presentation for a low score. You can only fail it for an undefended one.
