🎚️ The Seven Dimensions — tap a level per row

Score what you saw and heard, not who they are. 0 = not shown · 2 = solid · 4 = trainer-impressed. Hover a level to see what it means.

🧠 1 · Problem Understanding

Can they restate the challenge, the audience, and why the trap exists — in their own words, before showing any pixels?

🎨 2 · Design & Form of the Dashboard

Does the form fit the question? F-pattern layout, chart types chosen for the data shape, CVD-safe palette visibly applied, not a wall of gauges.

🔥 3 · Depth of the Grill

Do they show real grill rounds — dependent follow-ups that changed the spec? Numbered decisions D1…Dn? Can they quote a decision they revised after grilling?

💡 4 · Inferences

Beyond reading numbers off the screen: what does the data imply? Do they connect series to causes with appropriate caution?

🕵️ 5 · Detections

Did they find the trap(s) planted in the dataset — and name them precisely (truncated axis? missing baseline? colour-only encoding?)?

🌐 6 · Generalizations

What travels beyond this dataset? Do they say where their finding holds and where it breaks — sample limits, context boundaries?

✅ 7 · Conclusions

Do they land it: a clear recommendation to the audience they chose, tied to the evidence shown — or just "looks nice"?

🧭 Why these seven? (and how to use them honestly)

  • They mirror the method. Understanding → the grill's starting point. Design & Form → spec-before-pixel made visible. Grill depth → rounds + numbered decisions. Inferences · Detections · Generalizations · Conclusions → the four sensemaking moves the Challenge-Grill Method trains, and the exact things the trainer asks in the viva.
  • Audit trail, not a popularity contest. The per-dimension levels force you to notice what earned the score. A 2 with a written note beats a 4 you can't justify — same rule the presenters live by.
  • Peer scores inform, the trainer decides. Your scorecard lands next to their self-score and the trainer's — deltas between all three are the most honest picture of a presentation. Grade the performance you saw, not the friendship.
  • Use it on yourself too. Watching three pairs and scoring them will change how you rehearse your own 5–7 minutes. That's not a side effect — that's the point.
